GEMSTONE KNOWLEDGE

BLUE SAPPHIRE — The Beauty of Deep Blue

Blue sapphire is one of the most admired colored gemstones, known for its rich blue tones, exceptional durability and timeless appearance. Sapphire belongs to the corundum mineral family—the same mineral family as ruby—and blue coloration is associated primarily with trace amounts of iron and titanium.

At SAAS GEMS, careful cutting and polishing are used to reveal the individual color, brilliance and character of each stone.

Color: Blue to violetish blueMineral: CorundumMohs Hardness: 9Birthstone: September

RUBY — The Power of Red

Ruby is celebrated for its intense red color and has been treasured across cultures for centuries. Like sapphire, ruby belongs to the corundum mineral family. Its distinctive red color is produced by chromium within the crystal structure.

A carefully cut ruby can display remarkable depth, brilliance and personality, making every natural stone visually unique.

Color: Red to purplish redMineral: CorundumMohs Hardness: 9Birthstone: July

EMERALD — Nature in Green

Emerald is famous for its captivating green color and is one of the best-known precious colored gemstones. Its distinctive appearance can range through different shades and intensities of green, giving individual emeralds their own character.

Natural emeralds commonly contain internal features and inclusions. Rather than expecting every natural stone to appear perfectly flawless, these characteristics can form part of an emerald's individual appearance.

Color: GreenGem Family: BerylStyle: Elegant & TimelessKnown For: Rich green color

AMETHYST — The Elegance of Purple

Amethyst is the purple variety of quartz and is admired for colors ranging from delicate pastel violet to deeper purple. Once considered comparable in rarity to ruby and emerald, large discoveries made amethyst more widely available for jewelry and decorative pieces.

Its distinctive purple appearance makes amethyst an excellent choice for customers seeking an elegant gemstone with a bold but refined personality.

Color: Purple to violetMineral: QuartzBirthstone: FebruaryStyle: Elegant & Contemporary

DIAMOND — Timeless Brilliance

Diamond is renowned for its exceptional hardness, brilliance and enduring place in fine jewelry. Its ability to interact strongly with light gives a well-cut diamond its characteristic brightness and sparkle.

The beauty of a diamond is influenced by several characteristics, particularly its cut, color, clarity and carat weight. A carefully selected stone can become a timeless centerpiece for both classic and contemporary jewelry.

Known For: Exceptional brillianceMohs Hardness: 10Style: Timeless LuxuryKey Factors: Cut, Color, Clarity & Carat Weight

TOPAZ — A World of Color

Topaz offers an impressive variety of colors, including yellow, orange, pink, red, brown and blue. An important fact for buyers is that much of the blue topaz found commercially obtains its blue color through treatment rather than occurring naturally in that color.

With a Mohs hardness of 8, topaz is a relatively hard gemstone, although proper handling and storage are still important.

Colors: Blue, yellow, orange, pink and moreMohs Hardness: 8Appearance: Bright & BrilliantStyle: Classic & Versatile

GEMSTONE EDUCATION SERIES

1. HOW TO CHOOSE THE PERFECT GEMSTONE

Choosing a gemstone is about more than simply selecting your favorite color. Every stone has its own character, brilliance, clarity and natural features.

Start by considering the gemstone's purpose. A stone intended for everyday jewelry may need greater durability, while a collector may focus more on rarity, origin, color or unusual natural characteristics.

Color is often one of the first things people notice. Look at the tone, saturation and overall appearance of the stone under different lighting conditions. The quality of the cut is also important because skilled cutting can greatly influence how beautifully a gemstone reflects light.

Most importantly, choose a gemstone that genuinely appeals to you. A beautiful stone should feel personal rather than simply following a trend.

SAAS GEMS Tip: Compare several stones side by side before making your final choice.

2. NATURAL VS SYNTHETIC GEMSTONES: WHAT'S THE DIFFERENCE?

Natural and synthetic gemstones can look remarkably similar, but their origins are very different.

Natural gemstones form through geological processes within the Earth. They are mined, examined, cut and polished before becoming the finished stones we see in jewelry and collections.

Synthetic gemstones, sometimes called laboratory-grown gemstones, are created in controlled environments. They can have essentially the same chemical composition and crystal structure as their natural counterparts, depending on the gemstone.

Synthetic does not automatically mean fake. However, sellers should clearly disclose whether a gemstone is natural, laboratory-grown or an imitation.

When purchasing valuable gemstones, transparency about treatments and origin is an important part of making an informed decision.

SAAS GEMS Tip: Ask for clear information about whether a stone is natural, synthetic and whether it has received any treatments.

3. HOW TO CARE FOR YOUR GEMSTONES

Beautiful gemstones deserve proper care.

Although some stones are highly durable, gemstones differ significantly in hardness, toughness and sensitivity to heat, chemicals and sudden temperature changes.

Avoid exposing valuable gemstone jewelry unnecessarily to perfumes, cosmetics and household cleaning chemicals. Store pieces separately whenever possible so harder stones do not scratch softer materials.

Cleaning methods should also be selected according to the gemstone. A soft cloth and gentle cleaning may be appropriate for many stones, while ultrasonic or steam cleaners are unsuitable for certain gemstones or treatments.

When dealing with an expensive or unfamiliar gemstone, professional cleaning is often the safest option.

SAAS GEMS Tip: Never assume that one cleaning method is safe for every gemstone.

4. THE JOURNEY FROM ROUGH STONE TO POLISHED GEM

Before a gemstone reaches a display case, it may begin as an unassuming piece of rough mineral.

The transformation requires experience, patience and precision. A gem cutter first studies the rough material, considering its shape, color, inclusions and potential finished size.

The stone is then carefully shaped before facets are created and polished. Every decision can influence the final appearance of the gemstone.

An experienced cutter aims to find a balance between preserving valuable material and revealing the stone's color and brilliance.

This craftsmanship is why cutting is such an important part of the gemstone's story.

From nature to craftsmanship, every finished gem has a journey.

5. WHAT MAKES A GEMSTONE VALUABLE?

There is no single characteristic that determines the value of every gemstone.

Depending on the gem variety, important factors can include color, clarity, cut, carat weight, rarity, origin and treatments.

For many colored gemstones, color has a particularly important influence on desirability. Cut can affect how well that color and brilliance are displayed, while clarity expectations differ from one gemstone variety to another.

Treatments also matter. Some treatments are common and accepted within the gemstone trade, but proper disclosure is important.

This is why two gemstones of apparently similar size and color can have very different values.

SAAS GEMS Tip: Evaluate the complete gemstone rather than judging it only by size.

6. THE ART OF GEM CUTTING

Gem cutting is where nature meets human craftsmanship.

A skilled gem cutter does much more than create an attractive shape. Before cutting begins, the craftsman studies the rough gemstone to understand its structure, color distribution and natural characteristics.

The cutter then determines how the stone should be oriented and shaped. Faceting and polishing require precision because small changes can influence the finished gemstone's proportions and appearance.

The objective is not simply to make a gemstone sparkle. Good craftsmanship aims to reveal the best qualities already present within the natural stone.
